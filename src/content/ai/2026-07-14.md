# AI Digest — Monday, July 14, 2026
*Coverage window: previous 24 hours*

## TL;DR
Google dropped Gemini Omni and computer use in Gemini 3.5 Flash — two major capability expansions in one day. Mistral launched OCR 4 and a robotics navigation model. On the Bedrock side, three AWS posts show production-grade multi-agent and agentic QA patterns worth your team's attention. Plus: a Cursor 0-day via full disclosure that has implications for any IDE-integrated AI setup.

## What's New

- **[Introducing Gemini Omni](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-omni/)** — Google's new frontier model; no detailed spec in the source but the name signals a full multimodal replacement for Gemini Ultra. Combined with the Flash release, Google is pushing hard on the capability-cost curve this week. Watch how this lands on Vertex/Bedrock availability timelines for your teams. `[SHARE w/ engineering team]`

- **[Introducing computer use in Gemini 3.5 Flash](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-computer-use-gemini-3-5-flash/?utm_source=deepmind.google&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=gdm&utm_content=)** — Gemini now has a computer-use capability in its Flash tier, following Anthropic's lead. This is notable because Flash is cheap — if it's reliable, it makes browser/UI automation accessible at scale. Relevant to your agentic QA work. `[TRY]`

- **[Introducing Mistral OCR 4](https://mistral.ai/news/ocr-4/)** — Mistral claims state-of-the-art document intelligence. Directly relevant to your OCR/Azure AI Search pipeline for small business card underwriting. Worth benchmarking against your current stack — Mistral's EU data residency story may also matter for compliance discussions. `[READ DEEPER]`

- **[Multi-agent social intelligence with Strands Agents and Amazon Bedrock](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/multi-agent-social-intelligence-with-strands-agents-and-amazon-bedrock/)** — Production case study comparing Swarm vs. Graph orchestration patterns on Bedrock AgentCore, with head-to-head benchmarks on latency, cost, and output quality. The governance controls section is the most portable takeaway for your teams adopting agentic coding. `[SHARE w/ leadership]`

- **[Accelerating software delivery with agentic QA automation using Amazon Nova Act – Part 2](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/accelerating-software-delivery-with-agentic-qa-automation-using-amazon-nova-act-part-2/)** — Extends the Nova Act QA pattern to batch regression testing and CI/CD pipeline integration. Concrete architecture you could hand to a team tomorrow for a spike. `[SHARE w/ engineering team]`

- **[Cursor 0day: When Full Disclosure Becomes the Only Protection Left](https://mindgard.ai/blog/cursor-0day-when-full-disclosure-becomes-the-only-protection-left)** — A live vulnerability in Cursor disclosed publicly after the vendor didn't patch. The attack surface is IDE-level prompt injection. Any team using AI coding assistants (including your Claude Code + Bedrock setup) should understand this class of threat — the fix patterns apply beyond Cursor. `[SHARE w/ all teams]`

- **[5 Trends That Defined AI Engineering at World's Fair 2026](https://www.latent.space/p/aiewf26trends)** — Latent Space synthesis from AIEWF: the field has crossed from "building with agents" to "building systems around agents." The framing maps well to how you're thinking about cross-team agentic adoption. `[READ DEEPER]`

- **[Quoting Armin Ronacher](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/14/armin-ronacher/#atom-everything)** — "Before agents, some of this shared understanding was maintained by friction." Ronacher argues agents are eroding the informal shared language of codebases — the implicit knowledge that lives in code review and conversation. Short but sharp, and directly relevant to how you coach teams on AI-assisted development. `[READ DEEPER]`
