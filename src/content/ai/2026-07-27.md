# AI Digest — Monday, July 27, 2026
*Coverage window: previous 24 hours*

## TL;DR
AWS published two genuinely new document-AI posts today: a task-aware alternative to RAG for queries spanning hundreds of documents, and a Nova-based clinical document processing case study — both worth reading against your underwriting pipeline. Import AI covers a new benchmark (MirrorCode) showing AI still can't reliably finish week-long programming tasks, a useful gut-check on how much autonomy to hand agentic coding workflows. Everything else in today's feed — Opus 5, Gemini 3.6 Flash, Mistral OCR 4, Mistral Studio — is a repeat from earlier this week.

## What's New

- **[Beyond RAG: Task-aware knowledge compression for enterprise AI on AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/beyond-rag-task-aware-knowledge-compression-for-enterprise-ai-on-aws/)** — AWS's answer to RAG's ceiling on analytical queries spanning hundreds of documents: pre-compress the whole knowledge base into task-specific representations, cache them at multiple fidelity tiers, and route each query to the cheapest tier that answers it, with an open-source implementation included. Worth testing against the multi-document underwriting questions where your current RAG setup likely struggles. [TRY]

- **[How Guardoc transforms medical document processing with Amazon Nova models](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/how-guardoc-transforms-medical-document-processing-with-amazon-nova-models/)** — Case study on using Bedrock's Nova models to extract structured data from messy clinical documents in long-term care. Same shape as your card-underwriting ingestion problem (unstructured source docs → structured fields) even though the domain differs — the architecture notes are worth a skim. [SHARE w/ engineering team]

- **[Import AI 466: The bitter lesson for robotics, AIs complete week-long programming tasks; and OpenAI's accidental AI hacker](https://jack-clark.net/2026/07/27/import-ai-466-the-bitter-lesson-for-robotics-ais-complete-week-long-programming-tasks-and-openais-accidental-ai-hacker/)** — Epoch and METR released MirrorCode, a benchmark for long-horizon (week-scale) programming tasks. Models still can't reliably solve the hardest ones — a useful reality check on how much autonomy to hand agentic coding workflows across your teams right now. [READ DEEPER]

- **[An opinionated guide to which AI to use to do stuff](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/27/an-opinionated-guide-to-which-ai-to-use-to-do-stuff/#atom-everything)** — Simon's take on Ethan Mollick's guide, which has shifted from "which chatbot" a year ago to "which agentic system can do hours of unattended work" today. Useful framing for pitching agentic coding adoption to teams still thinking of Claude Code as a chat tool. [SHARE w/ leadership]

- **[moonshotai/Kimi-K3](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/27/kimi-k3/#atom-everything)** — Moonshot released the actual weights for Kimi K3 (2.8T params, 1.56TB on Hugging Face), following through on the tease from a few weeks back. Too large to self-host, but worth knowing another major lab has a frontier-scale open-weight model out.

- **[bradautomates/claude-video](https://github.com/bradautomates/claude-video)** — Gives Claude the ability to "watch" a video: downloads it, extracts frames, transcribes audio, hands it all to Claude. Trending at 11k+ stars. Quick to try if any of your workflows involve reviewing recorded demos or training content. [TRY]

- **[ChatGPT Voice is now in the desktop app](https://releasebot.io/updates/openai#ChatGPT-Voice-is-now-in-the-desktop-app)** — Voice mode rolling out to the ChatGPT desktop app alongside multi-folder project support. Consumer UX only, no action needed on your end.
