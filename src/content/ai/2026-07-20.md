# AI Digest — Monday, July 20, 2026
*Coverage window: previous 24 hours*

## TL;DR
Mistral shipped OCR 4, directly relevant to your card-underwriting document pipeline. AWS published two solid Bedrock production case studies — Couchbase's multi-model Claude architecture and Tradeshift's move from legacy BI to agentic AI (30x faster queries, 40% lower TCO) — plus a pattern for wiring agent workflows to business users via Amazon Quick + NVIDIA's NeMo toolkit. Google extended computer-use to the cheap Gemini 3.5 Flash tier, and there's a sharp piece on agent-swarm economics worth reading for your adoption pitch.

## What's New

- **[Introducing Mistral OCR 4](https://mistral.ai/news/ocr-4/)** — Mistral's new state-of-the-art document intelligence model. Direct competitor to your Azure OCR path for small-business card underwriting. No benchmark detail in the source — worth pulling the API docs and running it against your messier scans. [TRY]

- **[How Couchbase built a multi-model AI architecture for Capella iQ with Amazon Bedrock](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/how-couchbase-built-a-multi-model-ai-architecture-for-capella-iq-with-amazon-bedrock/)** — Architecture decisions and production tradeoffs behind routing across Anthropic's Claude family via Bedrock. Good reference if you ever weigh multi-model routing over staying single-model in your own pipeline. [READ DEEPER]

- **[Evolving from legacy BI to agentic AI at Tradeshift with Amazon Quick](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/evolving-from-legacy-bi-to-agentic-ai-at-tradeshift-with-amazon-quick/)** — Query response times up to 30x faster, 40% lower total cost of ownership, and embedded analytics turned into a revenue-generating product. Concrete numbers if you need a business case for agentic tooling investment. [SHARE w/ leadership]

- **[Build specialized agent workflows for your business with Amazon Quick and NVIDIA NeMo Agent Toolkit](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/build-specialized-agent-workflows-for-your-business-with-amazon-quick-and-nvidia-nemo-agent-toolkit/)** — Pattern for pairing Amazon Quick as a business-user front door with NeMo Agent Toolkit: a supply-chain risk example goes from dashboard + knowledge context to a guided mitigation recommendation. Useful reference for wiring agent workflows to non-engineering users. [SHARE w/ engineering team]

- **[Introducing computer use in Gemini 3.5 Flash](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/introducing-computer-use-gemini-3-5-flash/?utm_source=deepmind.google&utm_medium=referral&utm_campaign=gdm&utm_content=)** — Google adds browser/OS control to its cheap, fast Flash tier, extending the pattern Anthropic pioneered with Claude computer use. Puts price pressure on that capability across labs. [SHARE w/ engineering team]

- **[Introducing Gemini Omni](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/gemini-models/gemini-omni/)** — Google's new omni-modal model, alongside lighter Omni Flash / Nano Banana 2 Lite tiers. No spec detail in the source; noting for the record.

- **[Agent swarms and the new model economics](https://cursor.com/blog/agent-swarm-model-economics)** — Cursor's write-up on how running many cheap agents in parallel changes the cost equation versus one expensive model call. #1 on Hacker News (132 points, 57 comments). Directly useful for how you frame agentic-adoption ROI with your teams. [READ DEEPER]

- **[Reverse-engineering is cheap now](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/20/cheap-reverse-engineering/#atom-everything)** — Willison rounds up anecdotes of people using coding agents to reverse-engineer and automate undocumented devices, arguing agents collapse the ROI math on maintenance-heavy tasks that used to not be worth it. Useful framing for justifying agentic coding investment beyond greenfield feature work. [SHARE w/ engineering team]
