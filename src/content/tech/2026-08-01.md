## TL;DR

It was a substantive week for platform-level shifts rather than product launches: the EU forced Android open to third-party assistants (a real long-term win for Home Assistant), Apple confirmed its CEO succession with John Ternus taking over September 1, and researchers published a paper arguing LLMs can never be fully secured against certain attacks — worth a read given growing LLM tooling use. On the EV side, record global sales driven by high oil prices confirm the transition is accelerating outside the US even as domestic sales cool, and Honda's solid-state battery partnership with QuantumScape is a multi-year signal, not an immediate product. Apple hardware news was mostly "wait for fall" (Mac Studio refresh, iPad Air redesign) except for AirTags, which are back at their best-ever price. No urgent smart-home firmware or security issues this week.

## Tier 1 — Top Stories

- **[A big win for Android interoperability](https://www.openhomefoundation.org/blog/a-big-win-for-android-interoperability/)** — The EU ordered Alphabet to open eleven Android features — always-on wake word, ambient sensor access, screen automation — to third-party assistants on equal terms, after consulting Home Assistant's own Android developer. This is the regulatory lever that could eventually let Home Assistant's Android integration do things it's currently locked out of. [SHARE w/ leadership]

- **[iOS 27 breaks 15 years of muscle memory on iPhone and iPad](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/01/ios-27-just-broke-15-years-of-muscle-memory-on-iphone-and-ipad/)** — iOS 27, shipping this fall, changes how you open Notification Center for the first time since iOS 5 in 2011. Budget a few annoyed days re-learning the gesture when you update in September.

- **[How Apple Changed Under Tim Cook: 15 Years in Numbers](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/07/31/tim-cook-apple-15-years/)** — Cook ran his final earnings call this week; John Ternus officially takes over as CEO on September 1. Leadership continuity looks solid — an internal, product-side successor — which matters if you're deep in the Apple ecosystem for the long haul. [READ DEEPER]

- **['Largest Supply Disruption In History': High Oil Prices Drove Record EV Sales In 50 Countries](https://insideevs.com/news/803381/ev-sales-records-50-countries-high-gas-prices/)** — US EV sales are soft, but high gas prices pushed 50 other countries to record EV sales as they cut oil dependence. The global electric mobility transition is still accelerating — the US slowdown is a local story, not the trend.

- **[Honda has a new partner for the advancement of solid-state battery technology](https://www.engadget.com/2225030/honda-solid-state-battery-technology-new-partner-quantumscape/)** — Honda is partnering with QuantumScape on solid-state batteries ahead of an EV lineup overhaul. A multi-year roadmap signal, not something that should change any EV purchase decision today.

- **[A fundamental flaw leaves LLMs strikingly vulnerable to attack](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/07/30/1140927/a-fundamental-flaw-leaves-llms-vulnerable-to-attack/)** — Researchers presenting at ICML argue it's structurally impossible to fully secure LLMs against certain attacks, not a patchable bug. Worth surfacing to any team shipping LLM-backed tools — this shapes what guardrails you should expect to need permanently. [SHARE w/ engineering team]

## Tier 2 — Category Signals

- **[Home Assistant's New Device Database Is Here](https://smarthomescene.com/news/home-assistants-new-device-database-is-here/)** — A public, searchable database of compatible devices built from anonymous opt-in user data. Check it before your next Zigbee/Matter purchase — it's real-world compatibility data, not vendor marketing.

- **[Homey Expands Device Support With Matter 1.5 Update](https://smarthomescene.com/news/homey-expands-device-support-with-matter-1-5-update/)** — Matter 1.5 certification adds support for EV chargers, heat pumps, solar systems, and major appliances. Matter's scope keeps expanding past lights and locks into the energy-management devices you're more likely to actually want to automate.

- **[FireAvert joins Works with Home Assistant](https://www.home-assistant.io/blog/2026/07/28/fireavert-joins-works-with-home-assistant/)** — FireAvert brings the first gas/electric appliance shutoff devices into the official program. A legitimate new automation category — fire-risk shutoff — if you want to extend past convenience automations into safety ones.

- **[My Favorite Energy-Saving Home Assistant Automation](https://smarthomescene.com/blog/my-favorite-energy-saving-home-assistant-automation/)** — A solar water heater pump automation triggered by sunrise/sunset and live weather. A concrete pattern worth stealing if you're running solar or a heat pump and want weather-aware automation rather than fixed schedules.

- **[Top Stories: 'Apple Upgrade' Launches, iOS 26.6 Released, and More](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/08/01/top-stories-apple-upgrade-launches/)** — Apple launched its new "Apple Upgrade" hardware subscription program in the US alongside iOS 26.6. Worth a look if you'd rather smooth out iPhone/Mac upgrade costs than pay upfront each cycle; install iOS 26.6 as routine maintenance regardless.

- **[Apple's new AirTags are back down to their best price](https://www.theverge.com/gadgets/973973/apple-airtag-second-generation-deal-sale)** — A 4-pack of second-gen AirTags is $89 ($10 off) at Amazon/Target, matching the all-time low. If you've been meaning to tag bags, keys, or gear, this is the price to buy at. [BUY NOW]

- **[Mac Studio: Here's what Apple has in store for this fall and beyond](https://9to5mac.com/2026/08/01/heres-what-to-come-with-the-next-apple-mac-studio-refresh/)** — Apple is refreshing the Mac Studio this fall for the first time since early 2025 — the top-end model is still M3-based since Apple skipped an M4 Ultra. If you're eyeing a Mac Studio, the fall refresh is worth waiting for. [WAIT]

- **[iPad Air Could Finally Be Redesigned Next Year](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/07/31/ipad-air-could-be-redesigned-next-year/)** — The design hasn't changed since 2020; Apple is expected to move it to OLED in early 2027 with a new enclosure. If an iPad Air purchase isn't urgent, 2027's redesign is the one to wait for. [WAIT]

- **[Florida Got $200 Million For EV Chargers. It Wants To Spend It On Air Taxis Instead](https://insideevs.com/news/803441/florida-nevi-ev-charger-money-evtols-air-taxis/)** — Florida wants to redirect federal NEVI charging funds toward eVTOL air taxi infrastructure, despite eVTOLs having no FAA approval yet. A preview of how federal EV charging money is becoming a political football — buildout in some states may stall.

- **[After noise complaints, judge orders Waymo to stop overnight charging in Santa Monica](https://arstechnica.com/tech-policy/2026/08/after-noise-complaints-judge-orders-waymo-to-stop-overnight-charging-in-santa-monica/)** — A judge halted overnight charging of Waymo's robotaxi fleet in a residential area. A small but real signal that AV/EV fleet infrastructure will keep running into local-nuisance friction as it scales.

- **[Port22](https://www.producthunt.com/products/port22)** — A new tool for running Claude Code, Codex, and other coding agents from your phone. Worth a quick look if you or your ICs want to kick off or check on agent tasks away from a desk. [SHARE w/ engineering team]

- **[DeepSeek-V4-Flash-0731](https://www.producthunt.com/products/deepseek)** — DeepSeek released a new "Flash" model positioned as frontier-level agent intelligence at low cost. Another data point in the price/performance competition below the frontier labs — relevant if your teams evaluate model costs.

- **[Third-party app stores are about to take over Google Play. Here's how to prepare](https://www.androidauthority.com/third-party-app-stores-coming-google-play-how-prepare-3689912/)** — Regulatory pressure is forcing Google to open Android to alternative app stores. Context only — same interoperability wave as the EU Android decision above, but you're Apple-primary.

- **[Google plans to exempt sanctioned nations from Android developer verification](https://arstechnica.com/gadgets/2026/07/google-plans-to-exempt-sanctioned-nations-from-android-developer-verification/)** — Google will exempt users in sanctioned countries from its new Android developer verification requirement. Context only — a narrow carve-out in Google's broader sideloading lockdown.

Worth bookmarking next time you need a quick enclosure or bracket model for a smart-home project.
