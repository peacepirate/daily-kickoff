## Tier 1 — Core Reads

- **[The reality of being a principal engineer](https://leaddev.com/career-development/reality-being-principal-engineer?utm_source=leaddev&utm_medium=RSS)** — Cuts through the romanticized version of the principal/staff role: the actual job is leading people and influence, not primarily writing code. For you, this is useful calibration when you're setting expectations with ICs on the path to principal, or evaluating whether a current principal is delivering at level. The "lead people, not just code" framing is worth sharing verbatim in 1:1s with senior ICs who are chasing the title for the wrong reasons. `[APPLY]`

- **[Translate tech debt into business risk](https://leaddev.com/technical-direction/translate-tech-debt-into-business-risk?utm_source=leaddev&utm_medium=RSS)** — Frames tech debt translation as a manageable, repeatable practice rather than a one-off negotiation. In a regulated financial services context, this is especially powerful: debt that maps to audit exposure, incident likelihood, or compliance risk is debt that gets funded. Build a one-pager template with your TLs that maps specific debt items to concrete business outcomes before your next planning cycle. `[READ DEEPER]`

- **[Is Microsoft's EngThrive framework immune to Goodhart's Law?](https://leaddev.com/reporting/is-microsofts-engthrive-framework-immune-to-goodharts-law?utm_source=leaddev&utm_medium=RSS)** — Examines Microsoft's engineering metrics framework and whether it resists the classic failure mode: once a metric becomes a target, it stops being a good measure. Relevant if you're running DORA metrics or productivity tracking across your org. The core question to ask about any metric you use: "What's the fastest way an engineer could game this, and would I be able to tell?" `[SHARE w/ engineering team]`

Useful when you're framing headcount requests or advising your ICs on career positioning. The distinction between "AI engineering" as an emerging specialty vs. AI tooling augmenting existing roles is a conversation you should be having with your leadership peers now, before org design decisions are made upstream. `[READ DEEPER]`

## Action Buckets

**APPLY IN 1:1 or STAFF MEETING**
- In your next principal/staff IC 1:1, ask: *"What's the biggest gap between what you thought this role would be and what it actually is?"* — surfaces misalignment before it becomes attrition. Pairs with the LeadDev principal engineer reality piece.
- Before your next tech debt conversation with leadership, reframe one concrete debt item using business-risk language (e.g., "this increases incident probability by X, which maps to $Y of customer impact") rather than engineering terms. Pick the debt item your TLs are most frustrated about and build the translation together.

**SHARE WITH YOUR ORG**
- EngThrive / Goodhart's Law piece → **engineering team** — good discussion starter in a retro or TL sync: "Which of our current metrics could most easily be gamed, and are they?"
- 2026 job market data → **leadership** — context for headcount planning and AI engineering role definition conversations happening now across the industry.

**READ DEEPER**
- *State of the software engineering job market in 2026* — informs your hiring plan and how to frame headcount requests: AI engineering roles are growing but the picture is nuanced, not a simple replacement story. Read before your next workforce planning conversation.
- *Translate tech debt into business risk* — directly applicable to any upcoming planning or budget cycle. Build a repeatable translation template with your TLs; in financial services, compliance and audit risk framing unlocks funding that pure engineering arguments don't.
